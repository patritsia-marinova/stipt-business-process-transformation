# Business Analysis – STIPT

## Overview

This folder contains the business analysis conducted during the STIPT Business Process Transformation project. The purpose of this phase was to understand how the organisation's existing processes operate, identify inefficiencies, and investigate the underlying causes of operational problems.

The analysis focused on planning and scheduling activities, communication between employees and management, and the handling of customer complaints. I used business process modelling and root cause analysis to examine the current situation and establish a foundation for process improvement.

## Analysis Activities

### 1. Fishbone – Root Cause Analysis

[View Fishbone Root Cause Analysis](https://github.com/patritsia-marinova/stipt-business-process-transformation/blob/main/01-Business-Analysis/Fishbone_Root_Cause_Analysis.pdf)

The fishbone diagram was used to investigate the possible underlying causes of operational problems within the organisation.

Rather than focusing only on the visible symptoms, this analysis helped structure potential causes and examine how different factors might contribute to process inefficiencies.

The purpose was to understand why problems occur before proposing solutions. These findings provided a starting point for identifying areas that required further investigation and improvement.

### 2. IST Process – Bottlenecks & Wastes Analysis

[View IST, Bottlenecks & Wastes Analysis](https://github.com/patritsia-marinova/stipt-business-process-transformation/blob/main/01-Business-Analysis/IST_Bottlenecks_Wastes.pdf)

I used BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation) in Visual Paradigm to visualise the organisation's current workflow, including the activities, responsibilities, decision points, and communication between different stakeholders.

The diagram focuses on two operational processes:

**Planning & Scheduling**

This process shows how team leaders observe work locations, assign cleaning tasks, and communicate with employees when additional work or working hours are required.

The process involves several decision points, particularly when employees need to report unexpected situations or when additional working hours require customer approval. These interactions highlight potential bottlenecks related to communication, waiting for responses, and coordinating tasks.

**Complaint Handling**

This process illustrates how customer complaints are received by the account manager and communicated to the team leader for investigation and resolution.

The workflow includes identifying the issue, correcting the problem, informing the customer, and determining whether additional costs need to be charged.

The analysis highlights potential inefficiencies caused by repeated communication between stakeholders, dependencies on manual follow-ups, and delays in resolving customer complaints.

**Bottlenecks and Wastes**

By visualising both processes, I was able to examine where unnecessary activities, waiting times, and communication dependencies could affect operational efficiency.

The analysis helped identify opportunities to improve information flow, reduce unnecessary process steps, and support more consistent communication between employees, management, and customers.

## Outcome

The business analysis provided a structured overview of the current operational processes and highlighted areas where improvements could be investigated.

The findings form the foundation for the next stages of the project:

- **SOLL Process:** Designing an improved future workflow that addresses the identified inefficiencies.
- **GAP Analysis:** Comparing the current and desired situations to determine which changes are required.
- **Requirements Analysis:** Defining the business, user, and system requirements needed to support the proposed improvements.

These activities connect the initial business problems to potential process improvements and technical solutions.
